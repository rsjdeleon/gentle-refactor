I believe that sometimes, we define our happiness based on people, circumstances, or things—and we tend to hold onto them as if they’ll last forever. We worry about the possibility of losing them. Then, when change inevitably comes, we grieve. We attach to emotions as if they define who we are. If we dwell in regret or disappointment, it can feel familiar—even strangely comforting—to stay in that suffering.

**Letting go** is a profound idea. It’s easy to say, “just let go and move on,” but when you’re in the middle of it—especially when uncertainty hits—it can feel almost impossible. We fear losing our home, our job, our business, investments, our loved ones, even our health. Everything we value can be taken from us at any moment. And while we do our best to protect what we love, we must recognize that we have limits:

- We eat healthy and exercise to stay fit and enjoy our time, but over time, our health inevitably declines.
    
- We work hard and invest to support our families, but jobs, businesses, and investments can all be shaken by events beyond our control—like a pandemic or economic downturn.
    
- We love our families and nurture our relationships, but time is limited, and so are the moments we get to spend with them.
    

I’m not saying we should give up on these things—not at all. But when the time comes and we’re asked to let go, I believe it helps to have the awareness that we did our best to protect and nurture what we value. And that awareness can bring peace.

Sometimes, when everything feels stable—our income, our relationships, our daily routine—we don’t always notice how fragile life really is. We may take these blessings for granted. We might even start comparing ourselves to others, overlooking the good that’s already present. In trying too hard to hold onto what’s familiar, we can limit our ability to experience joy in the present. A moment can’t truly shine when we’re clouding it with fear.

Letting go doesn’t mean giving up. It means choosing to have **faith**—faith in the process, in yourself, and in something greater than what you can see.

> “Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the evidence of things not seen.”  
> — _Hebrews 11:1_

Sometimes it won’t make sense right away. But I believe that eventually, the reasons become clear. What we lose or let go of may be leading us to something greater than we imagined.

> “What no eye has seen, what no ear has heard, and what no human mind has conceived—the things God has prepared for those who love him.”  
> — _1 Corinthians 2:9_

There are many resources online that offer guidance on letting go—meditation, mantras, mindfulness, positive thinking. These can all help. But in my experience, **time** is one of the greatest healers. Whether you’re in a good place or going through a tough season, remember to savor the good moments with gratitude, and when bad moments come, trust that they won’t last forever. It may not feel that way now, but if you endure, you may come out of it stronger—and even wiser.
