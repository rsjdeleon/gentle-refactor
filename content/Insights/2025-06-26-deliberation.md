In my experience, it’s usually better to **calculate before reacting**. Acting hastily often leads to regret. I’ve found value in being still — waiting for more information, for events to unfold, or for things to settle. When the dust clears, I can prepare to act and speak with more clarity and wisdom.

Another thing that helps me regulate emotion is **using logic as a counterbalance**. Asking logical questions allows me to distance myself from emotional overwhelm. I find inspiration in the kind of reflection Marcus Aurelius used, like when he asked:

> _"Does what happened prevent you from acting with justice, generosity, self-control, sanity, prudence, honesty, humility, or straightforwardness?"_

Framing things this way makes me realize that even difficult events don’t have to take away my values or clarity — unless I let them.

It also helps me to **view situations objectively** — to see them as they are, without immediately inserting _myself_ into the equation. One trick I’ve tried is to imagine that the situation is happening to someone else. When I do that, it becomes easier to identify the right course of action, because the emotional fog clears a bit. It creates just enough space to think logically instead of impulsively.

I’ve noticed that it helps to shift from the **perceiving eye** (which is reactive and often exaggerated) to the **observing eye** — the part of me that calmly notices what’s happening without instantly assigning blame, shame, or fear. Our perceptions can distort reality, especially when emotions are running high. Being able to separate perception from reality is, in my opinion, a key part of emotional clarity.
