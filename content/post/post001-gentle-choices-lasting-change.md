## 🧭 On Decision Making and Risk

In my opinion, **making decisions** consists of gathering information about your options, evaluating potential opportunities and risks, learning from the experiences of others, and conducting an honest self-assessment of your strengths and weaknesses.

> A decision, at its core, is a commitment to a specific path or action in the face of multiple alternatives. It's a filter that determines where your time, energy, and values go.

**Making a decision is difficult.** Not all decisions can be quantified—we can't always measure all possibilities or values. Almost every option involves some level of risk that can significantly impact us.

Most of our choices aren't binary—**not simply right or wrong, good or bad, yes or no**. Many fall into the realm of uncertainty.

> Uncertainty means we don't have full control over the outcomes. Even with complete information, external factors—timing, people, luck—can change the result.

A decision might turn out well—or not. It may or may not lead to your desired outcome. That’s why it takes strength and **emotional resilience** to either pursue what we want or choose what’s necessary, even if it’s uncomfortable.

> Emotional resilience here refers to your capacity to move forward despite fear, doubt, or disappointment—being able to accept that the perfect decision doesn’t exist, but growth often comes from acting anyway.

But perhaps the **most dangerous decision is not making one at all**—because of _opportunity cost_.

> Opportunity cost is the potential benefit you miss out on when choosing one alternative over another.

Avoiding risk has its own risks. We operate with limited resources, such as:

- **Money** – It loses value over time due to inflation. While we earn through work, we also spend time and energy to do so.
- **Time** – I believe time is measured in actions and processes aimed at specific goals. Time is finite. We can run out of it unexpectedly—or live long lives—but either way, we all face the same end. It’s not a matter of _if_, but _when_.
- **Energy** – Our physical and mental energy declines as we age.
- **Other intangible resources** – like relationships, trust, goodwill, and social capital.

> When you delay decisions out of fear or indecision, you risk letting these resources quietly diminish without meaningful progress.

---

### 🏁 Conclusion

**Life is a risk—you’re not getting out of it alive.**

— _Jim Rohn_

At the end of the day, our time is limited. We don't always know how things will turn out—good, bad, or somewhere in between. But the more we choose, the wiser we become. And the wiser we become, the better we get at making decisions.

> Don’t wait for certainty—act with intention.

Taking action and having the courage to make the first move often leads to a better version of ourselves.
